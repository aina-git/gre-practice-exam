# GRE Ready question-content standard

## Purpose and claims

GRE Ready uses original practice material informed by ETS's public descriptions of the GRE General Test. It is an independent study aid. It must not describe its questions as ETS questions, POWERPREP equivalents, calibrated GRE items, or evidence that a test taker will meet an admission requirement.

The published GRE format, public skill descriptions, and official POWERPREP resources are the reference points for preparation. They do not disclose ETS's item bank, item parameters, adaptive routing thresholds, or score-conversion tables.

## Item requirements

Every published question should:

- Test one identifiable skill and provide all information needed to answer it.
- Have one defensible key (or the exact required set of answers) under the stated directions.
- Use distractors that reflect plausible reasoning errors, with a specific reason each is wrong.
- Avoid accidental answer cues, hidden assumptions, irrelevant factual knowledge, and ambiguous wording.
- Include an explanation that proves the key from the prompt and addresses the important competing choices.
- Use original language and contexts; do not copy, lightly paraphrase, or reconstruct recalled secure test content.

For verbal items, the sentence or passage must supply the evidence. Sentence Equivalence keys must both fit and yield equivalent meanings. Reading questions must be answerable from the passage, with inference questions not requiring unstated outside facts.

For quantitative items, independently recompute the answer from the displayed data, verify units and rounding, and check every distractor. Any generated parameter ranges must preserve those properties for every allowed value.

## Practice difficulty labels

Easy, medium, and hard are internal editorial targets only. A label should describe reasoning demand, not vocabulary obscurity or convoluted wording:

- **Easy:** one direct relation or inference.
- **Medium:** combine constraints or distinguish plausible alternatives.
- **Hard:** resolve qualifications, competing explanations, or linked inferences.

These labels are not equivalent to ETS difficulty levels until supported by controlled response data and an appropriate psychometric calibration process.

## Review before release

An automated check can validate structure, uniqueness of options, response format, and answer-key consistency. A separate answer-key-blind solve and a keyed critique can find some defects. These checks are useful screens, not proof of correctness or a substitute for qualified human review.

Before a larger bank is described as professionally reviewed, a reviewer should solve each item independently, verify its explanation and distractors, assess originality and content coverage, and record the review status. Quantitative generators should also be checked across their permitted parameter space. Keep rejected items out of student sessions.

After use, retain item-level response data only with appropriate privacy safeguards. Review item facility, discrimination, distractor selection, and subgroup behavior before making claims about difficulty or revising adaptive routes. Do not infer calibrated difficulty from a small or self-selected sample.

## Score reporting

Raw accuracy, pacing, and section-level practice routes may be reported as study feedback. A GRE-scale estimate must remain clearly marked uncalibrated unless it is derived from an adequately designed and validated calibration study. For an official practice benchmark, direct learners to [ETS POWERPREP](https://www.ets.org/gre/test-takers/general-test/prepare/powerprep.html).
